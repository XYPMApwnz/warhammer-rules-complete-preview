2. Profiles

Digital 11E

These contain the following characteristics that tell you how mighty the models in the unit are:

(M): The speed at which a model traverses the battlefield. If a model has an M characteristic of ‘-’, it can be set up on the battlefield but otherwise cannot be moved.

(T): The model’s resilience against harm.

(Sv): Presented as a dice result (for example 4+), this indicates the protection a model’s armour gives it.

(InSv): Presented as a dice result (for example 4+). Some models are protected by esoteric means in addition to physical armour, such as force fields or preternatural reflexes. Not all models have an InSv characteristic, but if they do, it will be listed here.

(W): Wounds represent how much damage a model can sustain before it is destroyed. If a model’s wounds are reduced to 0 or fewer, that model is destroyed.

(Ld): Presented as a dice result (for example 7+), this reveals how courageous, determined or self-controlled a model is.

(OC): How effectively a model can control an objective on the battlefield. If a model has an OC characteristic of ‘-’ it is unable to control objectives at all.

Modifiers
Digital 11E

WHAT ARE MODIFIERS?

Many rules in the game modify a value, characteristic or roll elsewhere in the game. A rule that does so is known as a modifier. A value that has been changed is a modified rule (for example, a modified characteristic, modified roll or modified value).

One of the most common ways for modifiers to be presented is as +1 or -1 to a characteristic, roll or value.

If a rule has +1 to a characteristic, it improves it by the value after the ‘+’ symbol. For example, ‘This weapon has +1 AP’ would improve an AP characteristic of -2 to -3.

If a rule has -1 to a characteristic, it worsens it by the value after the ‘-’ symbol. For example, ‘This unit has -1 Sv’ would worsen a Sv characteristic of 3+ to 4+.

When Modifying Characteristics

When modifying characteristics, unless otherwise stated, the following points also apply:

  • Characteristics of ‘-’, ‘*’ and ‘N/A’ can never be modified.
  • Rules that modify a model’s WS and/or BS characteristic modify the WS and/or BS characteristic of every weapon equipped by that model.
  • After all modifiers have been applied:
  • M cannot be less than 1".
  • T cannot be less than 1.
  • Sv cannot be 1+ or better.
  • InSv cannot be 1+ or better.
  • Ld cannot be 4+ (or better) or 9+ (or worse).
  • OC cannot be less than 0 or ‘-’.
  • cannot be less than 1".
  • A cannot be less than 1.
  • WS cannot be 1+ (or better) or 7+ (or worse).
  • BS cannot be 1+ (or better) or 7+ (or worse).
  • S cannot be less than 1.
  • AP cannot be worse than 0.
  • D cannot be less than 1.

When these terms are used in rules, the affected characteristics are being modified, and the following points apply:

  • Improving WS, BS, Sv and Ld: When improving a WS, BS, Sv or Ld characteristic, subtract the appropriate amount from the number before the plus sign, for example improving a WS of 3+ by 1 would result in a WS of 2+.
  • Worsening WS, BS, Sv and Ld: When worsening a WS, BS, Sv or Ld characteristic, add the appropriate amount to the number before the plus sign, for example worsening a WS of 3+ by 1 would result in a WS of 4+.
  • Improving AP: When improving an , subtract the appropriate amount from the characteristic, for example improving an AP of -1 by 1 would result in an AP of -2; improving an AP of 0 by 1 would result in an AP of -1.
  • Reducing or Worsening AP: When reducing or worsening an Armour Penetration characteristic, add the appropriate amount to the characteristic, to a maximum of 0, for example reducing/worsening an AP of -1 by 1 would result in an AP of 0; reducing/worsening an AP of 0 by 1 would result in an AP of 0.
  • Improving or Worsening Other Characteristics: When instructed to improve/worsen a characteristic that does not include a ‘+’ or ‘-’ symbol, add/subtract the specified amount to/from that characteristic (for example to improve a S by 1, add 1 to it).
Ignore Modifiers
Digital 11E

Some rules enable a player, weapon, model or unit to ignore modifiers. When using such rules, the following points apply:

Unless otherwise stated, that rule allows you to ignore modifiers to the unit’s rolls and the unit’s profile and weapon characteristics.

When a rule states you can ignore modifiers to a stated roll or characteristic, you can choose to ignore all of those , or only some of them. For example, you can still choose to apply positive/beneficial modifiers while ignoring negative/detrimental modifiers.

Example: Your unit can ignore characteristic modifiers and is under the effect of +2" M and -2" M. You can choose to ignore the -2" M, meaning that unit’s M characteristic will only be modified by the +2" M.

Example: Your unit’s ranged attacks can ignore roll modifiers and are under the effect of +1 to and -1 to hit rolls. You can choose to ignore the -1 to hit rolls, meaning those attacks will only be affected by the +1 to hit rolls.

Random Attacks: If a weapon has a random A characteristic, that characteristic is determined when generating attacks for that weapon at the step. If several weapons with random A characteristics are making , generate the attacks for each of those weapons individually, and group them all together.

When determining a random D characteristic, the dice roll made is called a .

Where a D characteristic includes an operator (for example a ‘+’, as in D6+1), the value after the operator is part of that D characteristic – it is not a modifier.

Other Random Characteristics: For all other characteristics, roll to determine the value on an individual, per-model or per-weapon basis each time that characteristic is required.

Healing Or Regaining Lost Wounds
Digital 11E

When a unit heals or regains a number of wounds, it regains up to that number of lost wounds. For each wound healed or regained, consult the following:

  • If that unit has one or more models that does not have its , select one of those models; that model regains one lost wound.
  • If all models in that unit have their starting number of wounds, but one or more models from that unit are currently destroyed, revive one of those destroyed models (excluding models), with one wound remaining.

This cannot cause a model to have more wounds remaining than it started the battle with.

If a rule states that a model heals or regains a number of wounds, only that model can regain wounds up to its starting number of wounds. Any excess regained wounds are lost and do not cause a destroyed model from that unit to revive.

Full Wounds Remaining
Digital 11E

A model has its full wounds remaining if it has the same number of wounds remaining as its W characteristic.

Rules With Multiple Conditions And Effects
Digital 11E

Some rules have multiple conditions, with the second, more specific condition conferring an improved rule, either instead of the first condition’s rule or as well as it. Such rules require both the first condition and the second condition to be satisfied for the improved rule to apply.

For example, an ability reads: ‘This unit’s ranged attacks that target the closest eligible target can:

  • Re-roll of 1.
  • Or:If that target is within range of an objective your opponent controls, re-roll hit rolls.’

In order for those attacks to re-roll hit rolls, that target must be both the closest eligible target and within range of an objective your opponent controls.

4. Weapons

Digital 11E

Weapons have the following characteristics:

(R): How far ranged weapons can shoot. Weapons with an R characteristic of ‘Melee’ are melee weapons.

(A): How many attack dice are used each time that weapon is used.

(BS): Presented as a dice result (for example 4+), this shows how accurate the is when shooting with the relevant ranged weapon.

(WS): Presented as a dice result (for example 4+), this reflects the bearer’s skill in wielding the relevant melee weapon.

(S): The higher a weapon’s S characteristic, the more likely it is to wound a foe.

(AP): Presented as a modifier to a dice roll (for example -1). The larger the modifier, the better the weapon is at cutting through the target’s defences.

(D): The amount of damage inflicted by an attack.

Weapons With No Strength
Digital 11E

If a weapon has no S characteristic (because it is ’-’, or for any other reason) and a rule interacts with that weapon’s S, that weapon has S 1 for that interaction.

5. Keywords

Digital 11E

Datasheets have a list of keywords, separated into faction keywords and other keywords. The former are used when deciding which models to include in your army, but otherwise both are functionally the same. Keywords appear in full capitals, in KEYWORD BOLD.

Some rules are linked to one or more keywords. For example, a rule might say that it applies to units. This means it only applies to units that have the INFANTRY keyword. Singular and plural instances of the same keyword function in the same way.

Using Keywords & Mixed Keywords in Units
Digital 11E

When using keywords, the following points apply:

  • A unit has all the keywords of all models in that unit. A model only has its own keywords.
  • If a rule specifies that it applies to a unit with a keyword, it applies to a unit which includes one or more models with that keyword.
  • If a rule specifies that it applies to a model with a keyword, it only applies to a model with that keyword.
  • If a rule specifies that it applies to ‘non-KEYWORD’ models/units, it only applies to models/units that do not have that keyword. For example, if a rule applies to non- units, it only applies to units that do not have the VEHICLE keyword.
  • Some units can contain models that have different keywords. While this is the case, such a unit is considered to have all of the keywords of all of its models, and so is affected by any rule that applies to units with any of those keywords. Remember that attacks are made against units, not models.
  • If a rule only applies to models with a specific keyword, then it instead only applies to models in such a unit that have the correct keyword.
  • A rule with a comma-separated list of keywords, or a series of keywords separated by slashes (‘/’), or two keywords separated by the word ‘or’ (for example ‘an IMMORTALS, DEATHMARKS or LYCHGUARD unit’) refers to a model/unit that has any one of those keywords.
  • A rule with multiple adjacent keywords (for example ‘an AELDARI GUARDIANS unit’) only refers to a model/unit that has all of those keywords.
  • Some older rules use keywords without specifying model or unit: for example, /VEHICLE or . These rules refer to units with those keywords.
  • Singular and plural instances of the same keyword function in the same way.
